**Hit Target** tells you, before a period ends, whether each device or asset will meet its target: stay under a
limit, such as a monthly energy budget, or reach a goal, such as a weekly production quota.

Part of the period is already measured. Trendz forecasts the rest, adds it to what is measured, and compares this
**predicted total** with the target. For example, a site forecast to use 366,000 kWh this month is over its
300,000 kWh budget, so it is **Off track**. Each item also gets a risk score, and can raise a ThingsBoard alarm
while it is at risk, so you can act while the period still has time left.

## When to use it

Use Hit Target when the question is yes or no by the end of a period: will each item stay within its limit, or
reach its goal?

| Use case | What Hit Target predicts | What you do with it |
|----------|--------------------------|---------------------|
| **Stay within the energy budget** | Whether each site ends the month under its 300,000 kWh budget | Act on the sites heading over budget while the month still has days left |
| **Meet the production quota** | Whether each line reaches its weekly quota of units | Move orders or shifts to the lines that will fall short |

The target is checked over the prediction period, so the period you pick in step 3 is also the deadline. If you
need the values themselves rather than a yes or no, use
<DocLink product={props.product} path="prediction/intents/forecast">Forecast</DocLink>. For a total over a period
without a target, use <DocLink product={props.product} path="prediction/intents/period-total">Period Total</DocLink>,
and for the moment a value crosses a limit,
<DocLink product={props.product} path="prediction/intents/time-to-threshold">Time to Threshold</DocLink>.

### Step 4: Define the target

The fourth step creates the first target.

<Aside type="tip">
Not sure what to specify? Leave the step empty and build the model without it. Targets can be added at any time in
[Targets](#targets) on the Result tab.
</Aside>

**Target name** — what the target is called on the Result tab.

**Aggregation** — how the values of the prediction period are combined before they are compared with the target:

| Aggregation | Behavior |
|-------------|----------|
| **Sum** | The total over the prediction period. |
| **Average** | The mean. |
| **Minimum**, **Maximum** | The lowest or the highest value. |

**Target condition** — what value the aggregated metric must reach:

| Mode | Behavior |
|------|----------|
| **Simple** | One comparison: **drops below** a limit to stay under, or **goes above** a goal to reach. The value is a **Value**, or an **Attribute** read per item. |
| **From alarm rule** | Reuses one severity of an existing ThingsBoard alarm rule. |

## Hit Target result page

The Result tab shows one target for one item at a time. Pick the item in the table below the chart; the cards on
the right show the monitoring settings and the data status of the model.

### Target card

The card on the left shows the selected target for the selected item. The selector at its top switches targets
when the model has several.

<ImageGallery images={[
  {

| Field | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **On track** or **Off track** | Whether the predicted total meets the target condition |
| **Risk** | **Low**, **Medium**, or **High** risk of missing the target |
| **Breaches on** | The day the running total is expected to cross the target, for an item that is off track |
| **Predicted total** | The measured part of the period plus the forecast for the rest, and its distance from the target |
| **Target** and **% of target** | The condition, and the predicted total as a share of it |
| **Period** | The period the target is measured over |
| **Alarm** | The severity of the raised alarm, or **No alarm** |

## Targets

A target turns the prediction into a yes or no answer. On every refresh, Trendz adds up what has been measured since
the start of the period, adds the forecast for the rest of it, and compares this **predicted total** with the
target.

<ImageGallery images={[
  {
    src: '/src/assets/images/trendz/prediction/target-projection.svg',
    alt: 'Diagram: the running totals of two sites from Sep 1, measured up to Now and forecast to Oct 1, against a 300,000 budget line; site-energy-003 crosses the budget on Sep 25 and ends at 122% of target, off track; site-energy-002 ends at 98.8%, on track',
    caption: 'Real numbers from the demo model: one site is heading over budget, one stays just under it.',
  },
]} />

A model can have several targets, such as a budget and a stretch budget on the same metric.

### Open the target editor

Click **Manage targets** under the target card:

<ImageGallery images={[
  {

    caption: '<b>Manage targets</b> opens the target editor.',

The editor lists the targets on the left and the selected target on the right. A model built without a target is

<Aside type="note">
**Manage targets** reacts to whatever the model is running:

- A **full model rebuild** — for example after you change the prediction method or another setting on the
  Properties tab — blocks the editor from opening at all.
- A **single item's refresh or retrain** lets you open the editor, but **Save** stays disabled until that item
  finishes.

Both are expected; the editor is fully available again once the run completes.
</Aside>

### Evaluation window

The evaluation window is the period the total is measured over, and **Aggregation** turns it into one number:
**Sum** for consumption, **Average** for a level, **Minimum** or **Maximum** for an extreme.

<ImageGallery images={[
  {
    src: '/src/assets/images/trendz/prediction/target-dialog-window.webp',
    alt: 'Manage targets dialog with the evaluation window highlighted: Use the model prediction period checked, the date range This Month, and the Sum aggregation',
    caption: 'Monthly budget: the sum over this month.',
  },
]} />

- **Use the model prediction period** keeps the target in step with the model.
- Clear it to give the target a date range of its own. A window spanning several calendar units adds **Window
  type**, **Sliding** or **Fixed** with an **Anchor day**.

### Trigger condition

The trigger condition is what counts as success:

<ImageGallery images={[
  {
    src: '/src/assets/images/trendz/prediction/target-dialog-condition.webp',

| Mode | Behavior |
|------|----------|
| **Simple** | Compares the predicted total with a **Value** or an **Attribute** of the item: **drops below** for a limit to stay under, **goes above** for a goal to reach |
| **From alarm rule** | Reuses one severity of an existing ThingsBoard alarm rule |

An **Attribute** gives every item its own target from its own attribute, with one target in the model.

### Business entity field and telemetry

Two actions make the % of target available outside the Result tab:

<ImageGallery images={[
  {
    src: '/src/assets/images/trendz/prediction/target-dialog-actions.webp',
    alt: 'Manage targets dialog with the Business entity field and Telemetry saving actions highlighted, and the telemetry key monthly_budget',
    caption: 'Telemetry saving writes the % of target to each site under monthly_budget.',

| Action | What it does |
|--------|--------------|
| **Business entity field** | Creates a field on the business entity with the % of target, for Trendz views |
| **Telemetry saving** | Writes the % of target to each item under the **Telemetry key** with the `_EPT_` prefix, one point per refresh. **Save telemetry** in the model's monitoring settings must be on as well |

A target whose value is zero writes nothing, because its share is undefined.

### Alarm creation

**Alarm creation** raises a ThingsBoard alarm of the **Alarm type** while the target is at risk. The type must be
unique among the rules and targets of the profile.

**Severity thresholds** — you define what counts as each severity: how much risk is a **Warning**, a **Major**, or
a **Critical**. They are off by default, so turn on the ones you need:

<ImageGallery images={[
  {
    src: '/src/assets/images/trendz/prediction/target-dialog-alarm.webp',
    alt: 'Manage targets dialog with Alarm creation highlighted: alarm type Monthly energy budget at risk and severity thresholds WARNING 80%, MINOR off, MAJOR 95%, CRITICAL 100%',
    caption: 'You set the alarm type and the risk at which each severity kicks in.',
  },
]} />

Risk reads the same way for both kinds of target, and higher always means more at risk:

- For a limit to stay under, risk is the % of target.
- For a goal to reach, risk is 100 minus the % of target.
- Risk stays between 0% and 100%.

The alarm takes the most severe level whose threshold the risk has reached, and clears when the risk falls below
every enabled threshold:

<ImageGallery images={[
  {
    src: '/src/assets/images/trendz/prediction/target-risk.svg',
    alt: 'Diagram: a risk scale from 0% to 100% with No alarm below 80%, WARNING from 80%, MAJOR from 95%, and CRITICAL at 100%; site-energy-002 at 98.8% is MAJOR and site-energy-003 at 100% is CRITICAL',
    caption: 'The thresholds of the demo model: WARNING 80%, MAJOR 95%, CRITICAL 100%.',
  },
]} />
