White labeling lets you brand your ThingsBoard instance: set the application title, logos, and colors, or add custom CSS, without restarting the service. The **White labeling** page has five tabs; customer users do not see **Mail templates**:

| Tab | What it customizes |
|-----|--------------------|
| **General** | Logos, titles, colors, custom CSS, and interface options. Described on this page. |
| **Login** | The login page on your custom domain: domain, logos, titles, and colors. See <DocLink product={props.product} path="user-guide/white-labeling-login">Login</DocLink>. |
| **Mail templates** | Email notifications, for example account activation and password reset. See <DocLink product={props.product} path="user-guide/white-labeling-mail">Mail templates</DocLink>. |
| **Custom translation** | UI languages and translation keys. See <DocLink product={props.product} path="user-guide/white-labeling-translation">Custom translation</DocLink>. |
| **Custom menu** | The navigation menu. See <DocLink product={props.product} path="user-guide/white-labeling-menu">Custom menu</DocLink>. |

## Inheritance Model

White labeling settings pass down from the system administrator to tenants and then to customers, including sub-customers nested in other customers. Each level inherits the settings of the level above and can override them for itself and the levels below.

To configure white labeling, a user needs the **White labeling** <DocLink product={props.product} path="user-guide/roles" target="_blank">permission</DocLink>.

<Aside type="note">
  A customer can use white labeling only if white labeling is allowed for the tenant and **Allow white labeling** is turned on in **Customer details** for that customer and every parent customer. For the Sub-Customer in the diagram above:
  - **Allow white labeling** is on for the Customer and the Sub-Customer.
  - The Sub-Customer's user group has the **White labeling** permission.

## General Settings

Go to **White labeling &#8702; General**. The tab has three sections: **Logo & titles**, **Appearance**, and **Interface options**. Customer users do not see **Interface options**.

Configure the settings in the sections below. Click **Preview** to see the result in the UI before saving, then click **Save**.

### Logo & Titles

| Setting | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Application title** | Title in the browser tab. |
| **Website icon** | Icon in the browser tab and bookmarks. PNG or ICO, 32×32 px recommended. |
| **Logo** | Logo in the upper-left corner. SVG or PNG, at least 96 px high, up to 200 KB. |
| **Logo height** | Logo height in pixels, up to 200 px for tall logos. When the field is empty, the default height shown in it is used. The logo scales proportionally and stays centered. |
| **Collapsed sidebar logo** | Logo shown when the sidebar is collapsed. Square SVG or PNG, at least 48×48 px, up to 100 KB. |
| **Override Trendz analytics Add-on name** | Renames the "Trendz" add-on to "Advanced analytics" across the platform. |
| **Show platform name and version in the bottom-left corner** | Shows the platform name and version in the bottom-left corner. Set them in **Platform Name** and **Platform version** (required; filled in with ThingsBoard and the current version by default). |

For **Website icon**, **Logo**, and **Collapsed sidebar logo**, click **Browse from gallery** to pick an image from the <DocLink product={props.product} path="user-guide/image-gallery" target="_blank">image gallery</DocLink>, or **Set link** to use an image URL.

### Appearance

**Application shell** sets how the primary color is applied to the sidebar, the app bar, and page headings:

- **Light** — white sidebar and app bar with colored accents.
- **Primary** — sidebar and app bar filled with the primary color.

**Primary palette** is the main brand color: the sidebar, app bar, page headings, buttons, links, and active menu items. **Accent palette** colors checkboxes, radio buttons, toggles, sliders, progress bars, and badges.

To set a palette:

<Steps>
1. Select a predefined palette from the **Primary palette** or **Accent palette** list, for example **Default**, **Red**, or **Purple**.
2. To adjust its colors, click **Customize** (pencil icon) next to the list. The **Edit palette** dialog opens.
3. The dialog shows the palette's 14 shades, from **50** to **900** and from **A100** to **A700**. Labels show where shades are used: **500** is the **Primary background**, **600** the **Secondary background**, and **300**, **800**, and **A100** are **HUE 1**, **HUE 2**, and **HUE 3**. Click a shade, pick a color or enter its RGBA values, and click **Select**.
4. Click **Save palette**.
</Steps>

<h4>Advanced CSS</h4>

Use custom CSS for what palettes and the application shell do not cover, for example icons, fonts, backgrounds, or scrollbars. The CSS applies across the whole platform.

<Steps>
1. Click **Add Custom CSS**.
2. Enter your CSS in the **Advanced CSS** editor.
3. Click **Save** in the dialog, then **Save** on the tab.
</Steps>

CSS set on the <DocLink product={props.product} path="user-guide/white-labeling-login">Login</DocLink> tab applies only to the login page.

## Reset to Default

To discard all customizations, click **Reset to default** at the bottom of the **General** tab and confirm.

<Aside type="caution">
  **Reset to default** deletes the white labeling settings saved at your level, and your level then uses the settings inherited from the level above. After you confirm, your customizations are lost and cannot be recovered.
</Aside>
