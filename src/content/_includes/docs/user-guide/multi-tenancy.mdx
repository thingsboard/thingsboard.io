Every <a href="/pricing/?product=thingsboard-cloud" target="_blank"><strong>Cloud subscription</strong></a> is a **Tenant** — an isolated environment that owns its own devices, assets, dashboards, rule chains, users, and other resources. Other tenants can never see or access your entities, dashboards, or telemetry, even though everyone runs on the same underlying infrastructure.

There's nothing to configure to get this isolation — it applies automatically from the moment your subscription is created.

<Aside type="note" title="Multi-tenancy on ThingsBoard Cloud">
  If you need multiple isolated Tenants, you have two options: create separate Cloud subscriptions, or switch to <a href="/pricing/?product=thingsboard-private-cloud" target="_blank"><strong>Private Cloud</strong></a> or <a href="/pricing/?product=thingsboard-pe" target="_blank"><strong>Self-managed</strong></a> deployment where you get full System Administrator control.
</Aside>

<ConditionalHeading level={2} id="customers-users-and-roles" showFor="paas,paas-eu">Customers, Users, and Roles in Your Subscription</ConditionalHeading>

Within your tenant, you can build out your own organizational structure and control who can see and do what:

<ul>
  <li>Create <DocLink product={props.product} path='user-guide/customers'>Customers</DocLink> to isolate data and delegate access to client organizations, regional branches, or internal departments.</li>
  <li>Create <DocLink product={props.product} path='user-guide/users'>Users</DocLink> and assign them to your tenant or to a specific customer.</li>
  <li>Scope access with <DocLink product={props.product} path='user-guide/roles'>custom roles</DocLink> and organize devices, assets, and dashboards into <DocLink product={props.product} path='user-guide/groups'>entity groups</DocLink> for fine-grained, group-level permissions.</li>
</ul>

<ConditionalHeading level={2} id="whats-not-available-on-cloud" showFor="paas,paas-eu">What's Not Available on Cloud</ConditionalHeading>

As a Cloud tenant, you don't have System Administrator access — you can't create additional tenants, manage tenant profiles, or change platform-wide settings. Those capabilities operate above the tenant level and are managed for you as part of the shared platform.

If you need multiple isolated tenants, you have two options: create a separate Cloud subscription for each one, or switch to <a href="/pricing/?product=thingsboard-private-cloud"><strong>Private Cloud</strong></a> or <a href="/pricing/?product=thingsboard-pe"><strong>Self-managed</strong></a> deployment, where you get full System Administrator control. See <a href="/docs/reference/architecture/deployment-scenarios/">deployment scenarios</a> for a comparison.

<Aside type="note">
Tenant data stays fully isolated even though tenants share the same underlying infrastructure — isolation is enforced at the platform level, not something you need to configure or verify yourself.
</Aside>

<ConditionalHeading level={2} id="what-is-multi-tenancy" showFor="ce,pe">What Is Multi-Tenancy?</ConditionalHeading>

Multi-tenancy in ThingsBoard enables a single platform instance to securely serve multiple tenants. A **Tenant** is a top-level logical entity that represents an organizational boundary — for example, an independent business, company, customer segment, or business unit.

Each tenant owns and manages its own devices, assets, dashboards, rule chains, users, and other tenant-specific resources, fully isolated from every other tenant on the same instance. See <DocLink product={props.product} path='concepts/multi-tenancy'>Multi-Tenancy & Hierarchy</DocLink> for a visual overview of how System, Tenant, Customer, and User relate.

<ConditionalHeading level={2} id="why-multi-tenancy" showFor="ce,pe">Why Multi-Tenancy?</ConditionalHeading>

Running one platform instance for many business units or customers means shared infrastructure costs and centralized administration, without sacrificing data isolation or security — each Tenant Administrator only ever sees and manages their own tenant's resources.

---

<ConditionalHeading level={2} id="isolation-and-security" showFor="ce,pe">Isolation and Security</ConditionalHeading>

Multi-tenancy in ThingsBoard is implemented through strict logical isolation:

<ul>
  <li>Each entity belongs to exactly one tenant.</li>
  <li>Permissions are enforced by <DocLink product={props.product} path='user-guide/roles'>role-based access control</DocLink>.</li>
  <li>Tenant Administrators cannot access resources belonging to other tenants.</li>
  <li>System Administrators have global visibility across all tenants.</li>
</ul>

This ensures secure tenant data isolation, even when multiple tenants operate within the same platform instance while sharing the underlying infrastructure. See <DocLink product={props.product} path='user-guide/security' target="_blank">Security</DocLink> for the password policy and JWT session settings that apply across all tenants.

<ConditionalHeading level={2} id="tenant-management" showFor="ce,pe">Tenant Management</ConditionalHeading>

Tenants are created and managed by the **system administrator**.

<ConditionalHeading level={3} id="create-tenant" showFor="ce,pe">Create Tenant</ConditionalHeading>

<Steps>
1. Log in to ThingsBoard as a **System administrator**.
2. From the sidebar, navigate to **Tenants**.
3. Click the **+ Add tenant** in the top right corner.
4. Enter a **title**.
5. Use the default <DocLink product={props.product} path='user-guide/tenant-profiles' target="_blank">tenant profile</DocLink> or select a different one.
6. Optionally, provide an address, phone number, contact email.
7. Click **Add**.
</Steps>

Once created, the tenant becomes available in the tenants list.

---

<ConditionalHeading level={3} id="edit-tenant" showFor="ce,pe">Edit Tenant</ConditionalHeading>

<Steps>
1. From the sidebar, navigate to **Tenants**.
2. Click the tenant row to open its details.
3. Click the orange **pencil** icon (**Edit**) to enter edit mode.
4. Update the tenant information.
5. **Apply changes**.
</Steps>

<ConditionalHeading level={3} id="delete-tenant" showFor="ce,pe">Delete Tenant</ConditionalHeading>

Click the **trash** icon next to the tenant and confirm deleting, or
open the tenant details page and click **Delete tenant**.

<Aside type="danger" title="Warning">
  Deleting a tenant removes all tenant-related data and entities.
</Aside>

---

<ConditionalHeading level={2} id="tenant-administrator-management" showFor="ce,pe">Tenant Administrator Management</ConditionalHeading>

A **Tenant administrator** manages all tenant-scoped entities and configuration for a single tenant — see <DocLink product={props.product} path='user-guide/roles' target="_blank">Roles</DocLink> for the full permission model. All operations are isolated within the tenant context and do not affect other tenants in the system.

<ConditionalHeading level={3} id="create-tenant-administrator" showFor="ce,pe">Create Tenant Administrator</ConditionalHeading>

<Steps>
1. From the sidebar, navigate to the **Tenants**.
2. In the **Tenants** list, click **Manage tenant admins** for **Tenant**.
3. Click **+ Add user** in the top right corner.
4. Enter the user&#39;s **email address**.
  Optionally, provide first and last name, and phone number.
5. Choose the user activation method:
    - **Display activation link** — generates an activation link that you can copy and share manually.
    - **Send activation mail** — sends the activation link to the specified email address (requires a configured <DocLink product={props.product} path='user-guide/ui/mail-settings' target="_blank">mail server</DocLink>).
6. Click **Add**.
</Steps>

After creation, the tenant administrator can log in and manage tenant resources.

<ConditionalHeading level={3} id="edit-tenant-administrator" showFor="ce,pe">Edit Tenant Administrator</ConditionalHeading>

<Steps>
  1. From the sidebar, navigate to **Tenants**.
  2. Click **Manage tenant admins** for **Tenant**.
  3. Click the Tenant administrator row to open its details.
  4. Click the orange **pencil** icon (**Edit**) to enter edit mode.
  5. Update the tenant information.
  6. Apply changes.
</Steps>

<ConditionalHeading level={3} id="enable-disable-tenant-administrator" showFor="ce,pe">Enable/Disable Tenant Administrator</ConditionalHeading>

ThingsBoard allows administrators to temporarily block a user's access to the platform without deleting the account.
Disabled users cannot log in or access any tenant resources.

To enable or disable a user account:

<Steps>
1. From the sidebar, navigate to **Tenants**.
2. Click **Manage tenant admins** for desired Tenant.
3. Click the User row to open its details.
4. Click **Enable/Disable User Account**.
</Steps>

  <ConditionalHeading level={3} id="delete-tenant-administrator" showFor="ce,pe">Delete Tenant Administrator</ConditionalHeading>
  
  Click the **trash** icon next to the tenant administrator user and confirm the deletion, or
  open the user details page and click **Delete user**.
  <Aside type="danger" title="Warning">
    Deleting a tenant administrator **permanently deletes** the user account and all related data.
    
    Tenant data — including entities, telemetry, etc. — is not affected and remains intact.
  </Aside>

<ConditionalHeading level={3} id="login-as-tenant-administrator" showFor="ce,pe">Login as Tenant Administrator</ConditionalHeading>

System Administrators can log in as a Tenant Administrator directly from the tenants list by using the **Login as tenant admin** action.

This is useful for troubleshooting tenant-specific issues and verifying tenant configuration.

<ConditionalHeading level={2} id="best-practices" showFor="ce,pe">Best Practices</ConditionalHeading>

To ensure stable and secure multi-tenant deployments:
- Use <DocLink product={props.product} path='user-guide/tenant-profiles' target="_blank">Tenant Profiles</DocLink> to enforce quotas and prevent excessive resource usage.
- Assign at least two Tenant Administrators per tenant for redundancy.
- Use consistent naming conventions for tenants and customer structures.
- Monitor message throughput and entity limits for each tenant.
- Apply consistent <DocLink product={props.product} path='user-guide/white-labeling' target="_blank">White-labeling</DocLink> per tenant to keep each deployment on-brand.

<ShowFor product={props.product} show={[Products.PE]}>

For enterprise environments with many users sharing devices and dashboards, replace the fixed roles with <DocLink product={props.product} path='user-guide/roles' target="_blank">custom roles</DocLink> and <DocLink product={props.product} path='user-guide/groups' target="_blank">entity groups</DocLink> to scope access to specific subsets of a tenant's resources instead of the whole tenant.
